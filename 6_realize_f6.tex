
\section{Recovering \texorpdfstring{$f_6$}{f6} from six-qubit AME signatures}
\label{sec:recover-f6}

By \cref{sec: AME62 has a common local unitary normal}, every
$\operatorname{AME}(6,2)$ state is locally unitarily equivalent, up to a
qubit permutation and a global phase, to the six-qubit hexacode state. We
now choose a Boolean representative of this orbit and prove the stronger
statement needed for the Holant reduction:
under the contraction hypothesis below, the standard signature $f_6$ is
realizable even though the six local unitaries are not initially available
as gadgets. We write $A\propto B$ when $A$ is a nonzero scalar multiple
of $B$.

Throughout this section, sums and exponents in Boolean variables are over
$\mathbb F_2$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \widehat f_6(x)=\mathbf 1_{x_1+\cdots+x_6=0}(-1)^{q(x)},\qquad
 q(x)=x_1x_2+x_1x_3+x_2x_3+x_1x_4+x_2x_5+x_3x_6.
 \label{eq:hat-f6}
\end{equation}
Its support consists of the $32$ even-parity strings.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:hat-f6-is-ame}
The normalized state $|\widehat f_6\rangle/\sqrt{32}$ is
$\operatorname{AME}(6,2)$.
\end{lemma}



The uniqueness result from the preceding section can now be expressed using
this concrete representative.

\begin{lemma}[Six-qubit AME normal form]
\label{lem:ame6-normal-form}
Let $g$ be a nonzero six-ary Boolean signature whose normalized state is
$\operatorname{AME}(6,2)$. Then there are one-qubit unitaries
$U_1,\ldots,U_6$, a permutation $\pi$, and $\lambda\in\mathbb C^\times$
such that
\[
 |g\rangle=\lambda P_\pi
 (U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_6)|\widehat f_6\rangle.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem: ame62 is unitary equivalent}, which identifies the unique
six-qubit AME orbit, both the normalized state of $g$ and the state in
\cref{lem:hat-f6-is-ame} are locally unitarily equivalent to
$|H_6\rangle$. Composing one equivalence with the inverse of the other
gives the asserted local unitaries; the two qubit permutations combine
into $\pi$.
\end{proof}

Recall that the fixed holographic matrix $K$ is unitary and satisfies
$K^TK=X$, where $X$ is the matrix of $\neq_2$. In the equality frame put
\begin{equation}
 f_6(x)=\mathbf 1_{x_1+\cdots+x_6=0}
 (-1)^{x_1+x_2+x_3+q(x)}.
 \label{eq:f6}
\end{equation}


Local-unitary equivalence alone does not supply the inverse local matrices
as binary gadgets. The next theorem upgrades the orbit statement to the
realization used later.

\begin{theorem}[Realization of $f_6$]
\label{thm:realize-f6}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a finite complex signature set that is
closed under entrywise conjugation and is outside condition
$\textup{(\ref{cond: tractable classes})}$. Suppose that
$f\in\mathcal F$ is nonzero and irreducible, its normalized state is
$\operatorname{AME}(6,2)$, and
$\partial_{ij}f\in\mathcal U^{\otimes}$ for $1\leq i<j\leq6$.
Then either $\operatorname{Holant}(\mathcal F)$ is
$\#\operatorname{P}$-hard, or there is an effectively computable
$O\in O(2,\mathbb R)$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{Holant}(=_2\mid\{f_6\}\cup O\mathcal F)
 \leq_T\operatorname{Holant}(=_2\mid\mathcal F).
\]
In the latter case the four Bell signatures
$\mathcal B=\{I,X,Z,J\}$ are available under the same reduction.
\end{theorem}

The proof has three steps. Pair contractions first restrict the relative
local matrices to a finite tetrahedral group. A common change of basis
then removes their shared continuous part. Finally, binary factors from
the contractions remove the remaining finite labels.

\paragraph{The tetrahedral filter.}
For $\epsilon,\eta\in\{\pm1\}$, put
\[
 R^+_{\epsilon,\eta}=
 \begin{pmatrix}\epsilon\mathrm i&\eta\mathrm i\\-\epsilon\eta&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 R^-_{\epsilon,\eta}=
 \begin{pmatrix}\epsilon\mathrm i&\eta\\-\epsilon\eta\mathrm i&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The twelve projective classes
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak T_X=K_4\mathbin{\dot\cup}
 \{[R^+_{\epsilon,\eta}]\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}
 \{[R^-_{\epsilon,\eta}]\},\qquad
 K_4=\{[I],[X],[Z],[J]\},
 \label{eq:tetrahedral-group}
\end{equation}
where both displayed sets range over $\epsilon,\eta\in\{\pm1\}$, form a
projective group. For $R_0=R^+_{+,+}$, the last two sets are
$[R_0]K_4$ and $[R_0]^2K_4$, while conjugation by $[R_0]$ cycles
$[X],[Z],[J]$. Hence
$\mathfrak T_X=K_4\rtimes\langle[R_0]\rangle\cong A_4$; in particular it
is closed under multiplication, inverse, and transpose.

\begin{lemma}[Tetrahedral filter]
\label{lem:tetrahedral-filter}
Let $Q$ be an invertible projectively unitary binary matrix. Directly
contract $Q$ into any two ports of $\widehat f_6$. The resulting four-ary
signature is a product of two binary signatures if and only if
$[Q]\in\mathfrak T_X$. Whenever it factors, both factors are
projectively unitary and have classes in $\mathfrak T_X$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Begin with ports $1,2$ and write
$Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The
remaining variables admit the pairings $34\mid56$, $35\mid46$, and
$36\mid45$. Factorization across one pairing is equivalent to the
corresponding $4$-by-$4$ flattening having rank one. For example,
\[
M_{34\mid56}(\partial_{12}^Q\widehat f_6)=
\begin{pmatrix}
a-d&b+c&c-b&a+d\\
b-c&a+d&a-d&-b-c\\
-b-c&d-a&a+d&c-b\\
a+d&b-c&b+c&d-a
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The $2$-by-$2$ minors of the three flattenings reduce, respectively, to
\begin{align*}
\mathcal I_1&=\{a^2+d^2,ab-cd,b^2+d^2,ac+bd,ad+bc,c^2-d^2\},\\
\mathcal I_2&=\{a^2+d^2,ab+cd,b^2-d^2,ac-bd,ad+bc,c^2+d^2\},\\
\mathcal I_3&=\{a^2-d^2,ab,b^2-c^2,ac,bd,cd\}.
\end{align*}
A list vanishes when all its entries vanish. Since $Q$ is invertible, the
flattenings are nonzero, so one has rank one precisely when one of these
lists vanishes.

If $\mathcal I_3=0$, invertibility gives either $b=c=0$ and $a^2=d^2$,
or $a=d=0$ and $b^2=c^2$; these are the four classes in $K_4$. If
$\mathcal I_1=0$, then $d\ne0$, and after scaling $d=1$ the solutions are
$(a,b,c)=(\epsilon\mathrm i,\eta\mathrm i,-\epsilon\eta)$, namely the
$R^+_{\epsilon,\eta}$. Similarly, $\mathcal I_2=0$ gives
$(a,b,c)=(\epsilon\mathrm i,\eta,-\epsilon\eta\mathrm i)$, namely the
$R^-_{\epsilon,\eta}$. Substitution proves the converse.

For a binary matrix $A$, write $A^{rs}$ when its two variables occupy
ports $r,s$. The same substitution identifies the factors:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
Q&\partial_{12}^Q\widehat f_6\\ \hline
I&2X^{36}X^{45}\\
X&2J^{36}Z^{45}\\
Z&2Z^{36}I^{45}\\
J&-2I^{36}J^{45}\\
R^+_{\epsilon,\eta}&
 (1-\epsilon\mathrm i)(R^-_{\epsilon,\epsilon\eta})^{34}
 (R^+_{\epsilon,\eta})^{56}\\
R^-_{\epsilon,\eta}&
 (1-\epsilon\mathrm i)(R^-_{\epsilon,-\eta})^{35}
 (R^+_{\epsilon,-\epsilon\eta})^{46}.
\end{array}
\]
Thus both factors again lie projectively in $\mathfrak T_X$. They are
projectively unitary because the Klein representatives are unitary and
$R^\pm_{\epsilon,\eta}(R^\pm_{\epsilon,\eta})^\dagger=2I$.

For another contracted pair $ij$, list the three pairings of the remaining
ports lexicographically. An entry $rst$ below means that their rank-one
conditions are $\mathcal I_r,\mathcal I_s,\mathcal I_t$:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
ij&12&13&14&15&16&23&24&25\\ \hline
 &123&132&312&213&231&312&213&312
\end{array}\\[1mm]
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
ij&26&34&35&36&45&46&56\\ \hline
 &213&231&213&312&312&132&123
\end{array}
\end{gathered}
\]
The solution set is therefore unchanged. The corresponding factors differ
from those above only by transposition and left or right multiplication by
Klein elements, under which $\mathfrak T_X$ is closed.

If a physical binary gadget $B$ is attached through two surrounding
$\neq_2$ signatures, its effective cap is $XBX$ (or $XB^TX$ when its
ports are reversed). Since $[X]\in K_4$, the conclusion is equivalently
$[B]\in\mathfrak T_X$. This reconciles the direct-cap statement with the
bipartite gadget convention.
\end{proof}

The filter controls each pair separately. The next lemma shows that these
pairwise restrictions are compatible: all six matrices share one continuous
frame, leaving only finite tetrahedral labels.

\begin{lemma}[Common frame]
\label{lem:f6-common-frame}
Let $U_1,\ldots,U_6$ be unitary matrices satisfying
$[U_i^TXU_j]\in\mathfrak T_X$ whenever $i\ne j$. Then there are a
unitary matrix $W$ and matrices $C_1,\ldots,C_6$ such that
\[
 W^TXW=X,\qquad X\overline WX=W,\qquad
 W^{-1}U_i=C_i,\quad [C_i]\in\mathfrak T_X.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take $U_1$ as reference and put $D=U_1^TXU_1$ and
$B_i=U_1^TXU_i$ for $i>1$. For distinct $i,j>1$,
$U_i^TXU_j=B_i^TD^{-1}B_j$. Indeed,
$D^{-1}=U_1^{-1}XU_1^{-T}$, and the intervening copies of
$U_1,U_1^{-1}$ and $X^2$ cancel. Rearranging this identity expresses
$D^{-1}$ using $B_i^T,U_i^TXU_j,B_j$ and their inverses. Their classes
belong to $\mathfrak T_X$, so $[D^{-1}]\in\mathfrak T_X$.

Set $R=U_1D^{-1}$, $A_1=D$, and $A_i=B_i$ for $i>1$. Then
$U_i=RA_i$ and $[A_i]\in\mathfrak T_X$. Since the $U_i$ are unitary and
the $A_i$ projectively unitary, $R$ is projectively unitary. Rescale $R$,
with the inverse rescaling of every $A_i$, so that $R$ is unitary.

The matrix $S=R^TXR$ is symmetric and unitary. As $D$ is symmetric,
direct calculation gives $S=D^{-1}$, hence $[S]\in\mathfrak T_X$.
The only symmetric classes in \eqref{eq:tetrahedral-group} are represented
by $I,X,Z$; a phase rescaling of $R$ makes $S$ equal to one of them.
Choose
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
S&A_0\\ \hline
X&I\\[1mm]
I&\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm i}{\sqrt2}
 \begin{pmatrix}\mathrm i&\mathrm i\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\\[4mm]
Z&\displaystyle\frac{1-\mathrm i}{2}
 \begin{pmatrix}\mathrm i&1\\-\mathrm i&1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{array}
\]
In each row, $A_0^\dagger A_0=I$, $A_0^TSA_0=X$, and
$[A_0]\in\mathfrak T_X$. Let $W=RA_0$ and
$C_i=A_0^{-1}A_i$. Then $W$ is unitary, $W^TXW=X$, and
$W^{-1}U_i=C_i$ with $[C_i]\in\mathfrak T_X$.

Finally, $W^TXW=X$ gives $W^{-1}=XW^TX$. Comparing this with
$W^{-1}=W^\dagger=\overline W^{\,T}$ and transposing yields
$X\overline WX=W$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Removing the finite labels.}
The common-frame lemma leaves six matrices in $\mathfrak T_X$. We now pass
to the equality frame, where extracted binary factors compose directly on
equality edges. Write
\[
 \mathfrak T_{=}=K\mathfrak T_XK^{-1},\qquad
 F_0=I,\quad F_1=\mathrm iX,\quad F_2=J,\quad F_3=\mathrm iZ.
\]
The classes of the $F_s$ form the normal Klein subgroup of
$\mathfrak T_{=}$. The other eight classes have representatives
\[
 r_{\epsilon_1\epsilon_2\epsilon_3}
 =\tfrac12(F_0+\epsilon_1F_1+\epsilon_2F_2+\epsilon_3F_3),
 \qquad \epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3\in\{\pm1\},
\]
and $\mathfrak T_{=}/K_4\cong\mathbb Z_3$.

Using the preceding port notation, the tensor
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 g_\star=\frac12\big(&F_0^{12}F_0^{36}F_0^{45}
 +F_1^{12}F_3^{36}F_2^{45}\\
 &+F_2^{12}F_1^{36}F_3^{45}
 +F_3^{12}F_2^{36}F_1^{45}\big)
\end{split}
\label{eq:gstar-four-bell-terms}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 g_\star=\tfrac12
 (I\otimes I\otimes(-F_2)\otimes X\otimes I\otimes I)f_6.
 \label{eq:gstar-to-f6}
\end{equation}
Thus recovering $g_\star$ is equivalent to recovering $f_6$ once the Bell
signatures are available. The next lemma performs this finite cancellation.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-label cancellation]
\label{lem:f6-residual-labels}
Let $\mathcal E$ be a finite algebraic signature family that is conjugate
closed and outside condition
$\textup{(\ref{cond: tractable classes})}$. Suppose that $\mathcal E$
realizes
\[
 g\propto(B_1\otimes\cdots\otimes B_6)g_\star,\qquad
 [B_i]\in\mathfrak T_{=}.
\]
Then either $\operatorname{Holant}(\mathcal E)$ is
$\#\operatorname{P}$-hard, or $f_6$ and the four members of $\mathcal B$
can be adjoined to $\mathcal E$ by a polynomial-time Turing reduction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Contracting ports $i,j$ of $g$ by equality inserts $B_i^TB_j$ into the
same ports of $g_\star$. After conjugating by $K$ and using
\eqref{eq:gstar-to-f6}, this becomes an $X$-contraction of
$\widehat f_6$ with an effective cap in $\mathfrak T_X$. By
\cref{lem:tetrahedral-filter}, it is a product of two
projectively unitary binaries whose classes, after restoring the four
exterior labels, lie in $\mathfrak T_{=}$.

Apply \cref{thm:paired-entrance}(ii), which extracts the prime factors of
a reducible realizable signature unless hardness already follows, to all
fifteen contractions. If one application gives hardness, we are done.
Otherwise all resulting binary factors, their conjugates, and their
transposes may be adjoined.
Let $\mathfrak G\leq\mathfrak T_{=}$ be the projective group they generate.
If $\mathfrak G$ contains a class outside $K_4$, then it contains that
class and its transpose. These belong to distinct order-three subgroups of
$A_4$ and generate $\mathfrak T_{=}$. Thus
$\mathfrak G=\mathfrak T_{=}$ in this case.

It remains to consider $\mathfrak G\leq K_4$. Put $r=r_{+++}$ and record
the coset of $B_i$ by $c_i\in\mathbb Z_3$, so that
$[B_i]K_4=r^{c_i}K_4$. Only differences of the $c_i$ occur, and we set
$c_1=0$. Direct multiplication in \eqref{eq:gstar-four-bell-terms} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
ij&c_j-c_i=0&c_j-c_i=1&c_j-c_i=2\\ \hline
12&36\mid45;(0,0)&34\mid56;(2,1)&35\mid46;(2,1)\\
13&25\mid46;(0,0)&24\mid56;(2,2)&26\mid45;(2,1).
\end{array}
\label{eq:two-contraction-quotients}
\end{equation}
Here $ab\mid cd;(u,v)$ says that the contraction factors on $ab\mid cd$
and that the two factors contributed by $g_\star$ have quotient classes
$u,v$. After restoring the exterior labels, their classes are
$c_a+u-c_b$ and $c_c+v-c_d$. Both vanish because every extracted factor
lies in $K_4$.

Solving these four equations gives the normalized words
\begin{equation}
 000000,\quad001221,\quad010212,\quad012120,\quad021102,\quad022011.
 \label{eq:normalized-coset-words}
\end{equation}
Allowing a common shift gives eighteen words: the three constant words and
the three cyclic assignments to each of
\[
 12\mid36\mid45,\quad13\mid25\mid46,\quad14\mid23\mid56,\quad
 15\mid26\mid34,\quad16\mid24\mid35.
\]

In the next display, $(M_1,\ldots,M_6)g_\star$ abbreviates
$(M_1\otimes\cdots\otimes M_6)g_\star$. The following three projective
symmetries explain this list:
\begin{align}
 &(I,I,I,F_3,F_3,I)g_\star
   \propto P_{(4,2,5,6,1,3)}g_\star,\label{eq:gstar-pairing-cycle}\\
 &(r_{+++},r_{+++},r_{+++},r_{+++},r_{+++},r_{-+-})g_\star
   \propto P_{(4,6,3,5,2,1)}g_\star,\label{eq:gstar-common-shift}\\
 &(I,I,r_{+++},r_{+-+},r_{+-+},r_{+--})g_\star
   \propto P_{(6,5,4,2,3,1)}g_\star.
 \label{eq:gstar-base-word}
\end{align}
The first cycles the five displayed pairings, the second adds a common
shift, and the third gives the word $001221$. They therefore cover all
eighteen possibilities. Consequently, for the actual coset word there
are $S_i\in\mathfrak T_{=}$ and a permutation $\pi$ such that
$[S_i]K_4=[B_i]K_4$ for every $i$ and
$(S_1\otimes\cdots\otimes S_6)g_\star\propto P_\pi g_\star$.

It remains to see that the extracted factors generate all of $K_4$, rather
than a proper subgroup. Projectively write $B_i=F_{\alpha_i}r^{c_i}$,
where $F_{00}=F_0,F_{10}=F_1,F_{01}=F_2,F_{11}=F_3$. Conjugation by
$r$ acts on $\mathbb F_2^2$ by
$\rho(1,0)=(1,1)$ and $\rho(0,1)=(1,0)$, so multiplication is
$(\alpha,c)(\beta,d)=(\alpha+\rho^c\beta,c+d)$.

Let $p_{ij}^{ab}$ be the oriented factor on $ab$ extracted from the
contraction on $ij$. For the two representative words, the multiplication
rule gives
\begin{align*}
000000:\quad
 &p_{12}^{45}p_{13}^{25}p_{23}^{14}\propto F_1,\\
 &p_{12}^{36}p_{12}^{45}p_{13}^{25}p_{14}^{23}
  p_{23}^{14}p_{24}^{16}\propto F_2;\\[1mm]
001221:\quad
 &p_{12}^{36}p_{14}^{26}p_{24}^{13}\propto F_1,\\
 &p_{12}^{36}p_{12}^{45}p_{13}^{24}p_{14}^{26}
  p_{23}^{15}p_{24}^{13}\propto F_2.
\end{align*}
These are binary chains on equality edges, and the unknown $\alpha_i$
cancel in every line. The symmetries
\eqref{eq:gstar-pairing-cycle}--\eqref{eq:gstar-base-word} transport
the identities to every allowed word. Hence
$[F_1],[F_2]\in\mathfrak G$, and these classes generate $K_4$. We have
proved
\[
 \mathfrak G=K_4\qquad\text{or}\qquad
 \mathfrak G=\mathfrak T_{=}.
\]
In either case the four Bell classes, and therefore the fixed
representatives in $\mathcal B$ after scalar normalization, are available.

If $\mathfrak G=\mathfrak T_{=}$, attach $B_i^{-1}$ to the six ports of
$g$ to obtain $g_\star$. If $\mathfrak G=K_4$, then
$[S_iB_i^{-1}]\in K_4$ for the $S_i$ chosen above. Attaching these Bell
filters replaces the labels $B_i$ by $S_i$ and produces
$P_\pi g_\star$; relabeling gives $g_\star$. Finally,
\eqref{eq:gstar-to-f6} yields $f_6$ using fixed Bell filters.
\end{proof}

We now assemble the three steps. The proof explicitly tracks the edge
matrix, so a direct effective cap is not confused with a physical binary
gadget connected through disequalities.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:realize-f6}]
Set $\widehat f=(K^{-1})^{\otimes6}f$. Unitarity of $K$ preserves
irreducibility and the $\operatorname{AME}(6,2)$ property. Moreover,
$K^TK=X$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \partial_{ij}f=K^{\otimes4}\partial_{ij}^{X}\widehat f.
 \label{eq:contraction-change-of-frame}
\end{equation}
Thus the contraction hypothesis is equivalent to
$\partial_{ij}^{X}\widehat f\in\mathcal U^{\otimes}$ for all $i<j$.

By \cref{lem:ame6-normal-form}, after relabeling the ports and absorbing a
nonzero scalar, there are unitaries $U_1,\ldots,U_6$ such that
$\widehat f\propto(U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_6)\widehat f_6$.
Capping ports $i,j$ by $X$ inserts the effective matrix
$Q_{ij}=U_i^TXU_j$ into $\widehat f_6$:
\[
 \partial_{ij}^{X}\widehat f\propto
 \left(\bigotimes_{k\ne i,j}U_k\right)
 \partial_{ij}^{Q_{ij}}\widehat f_6.
\]
The four exterior unitaries preserve both factorization and projective
unitarity. Hence \cref{lem:tetrahedral-filter} gives
$[U_i^TXU_j]\in\mathfrak T_X$ for every $i\ne j$.

Apply \cref{lem:f6-common-frame}. Its matrix $W$ is a legal uniform
change of basis in the hatted frame: $W^TXW=X$ implies
$W^{-1}XW^{-T}=X$, and $X\overline WX=W$ gives compatibility with arrow
reversal. Hence
\[
 \widehat h:=(W^{-1})^{\otimes6}\widehat f
 \propto(C_1\otimes\cdots\otimes C_6)\widehat f_6,\qquad
 [C_i]\in\mathfrak T_X.
\]

Return to the equality frame by setting $O=KW^{-1}K^{-1}$. The identities
$\overline K=KX$, $\overline{K^{-1}}=XK^{-1}$, and
$X\overline{W^{-1}}X=W^{-1}$ give $\overline O=O$. Also
$W^{-T}XW^{-1}=X$ and $K^TK=X$ give
\[
 O^TO=K^{-T}W^{-T}XW^{-1}K^{-1}=K^{-T}XK^{-1}=I.
\]
Thus $O\in O(2,\mathbb R)$.

Let $h=O^{\otimes6}f$ and $D_i=KC_iK^{-1}$. Returning the preceding
identity to the equality frame gives
\[
 h\propto(D_1\otimes\cdots\otimes D_6)f_6,\qquad
 [D_i]\in\mathfrak T_{=}.
\]
If $(L_1,\ldots,L_6)=(I,I,-F_2,X,I,I)$, then
\eqref{eq:gstar-to-f6} gives
$f_6=2(L_1^{-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes L_6^{-1})g_\star$. Thus, with
$B_i=D_iL_i^{-1}$,
\[
 h\propto(B_1\otimes\cdots\otimes B_6)g_\star,\qquad
 [B_i]\in\mathfrak T_{=}.
\]

Set $\mathcal E=O\mathcal F$. Because $O$ is real orthogonal,
$\mathcal E$ remains conjugate closed and outside condition
$\textup{(\ref{cond: tractable classes})}$, while equality edges are
unchanged. Since $h\in\mathcal E$,
\cref{lem:f6-residual-labels} gives hardness or jointly realizes
$f_6$ and $\mathcal B$ over $\mathcal E$. In the latter case,
\[
 \operatorname{Holant}(=_2\mid\{f_6\}\cup O\mathcal F)
 \leq_T\operatorname{Holant}(=_2\mid\mathcal F),
\]
as required.

Finally, $O$ is effective. There are finitely many port permutations and
choices of the six classes $[C_i]\in\mathfrak T_X$. For each choice, the
normal-form and common-frame identities form a fixed finite polynomial
system over the real algebraic numbers after separating real and imaginary
parts. At least one system is feasible by the preceding argument, and
standard real-algebraic elimination returns a sample solution. All matrices
and scalar normalizations can therefore be fixed before the input instance
is given.
\end{proof}
